Anomalous Voltage Responses Preceding Thermal Runaway in Cylindrical Lithium‑Ion Batteries

A multi-technique investigation of unusual open-circuit-voltage drops and recovery before rapid thermal runaway, combining ARC with X-ray CT, DSC, TGA and XRD.

Tianli Shi · Department of Chemical Engineering · University College London · Supervisor: Alex Rettie
Research question

Can voltage reveal failure before temperature does?

The study focuses on a fully charged B18650CX‑20M cylindrical lithium-ion cell and examines whether discrete terminal-voltage anomalies can act as earlier indicators of internal failure as the cell approaches thermal runaway.

Core experiment

ARC reveals two voltage drops and a non-monotonic recovery

Drop 1 · ≈19.6 h · ≈110 °C

Voltage falls from about 3.98 V to 2.45–2.50 V without a comparably sharp synchronous temperature rise.

Drop 2 · ≈23.6 h · ≈125–130 °C

Voltage falls from about 2.25 V to roughly 0.20 V.

Recovery · ≈23.7–26.2 h

Terminal voltage recovers toward 3.4–3.5 V while self-heating accelerates, making a simple permanent hard short an incomplete explanation.

Data-quality boundary: post-ADC-overload voltage is treated qualitatively rather than as a reliable quantitative measurement.
Structural context

X-ray CT maps the top-cap protection architecture

The CT cross-section identifies the positive terminal cap, top cover plate, can crimp, sealing gasket, pressure-relief disk/CID region, tentative PTC element, positive current-collector tab, jelly roll and steel can.

The CT image provides structural context for hypotheses involving pressure-driven or transient electrical disconnection, but it does not by itself prove CID activation during the ARC experiment.
Thermal characterisation

DSC: stepped heating, isothermal holds and cooling response

The DSC program spans approximately 95–120 °C and separates programmed temperature history from raw differential heat-flow behaviour, covering the same broad temperature range as the early ARC voltage anomaly.

These DSC data support temperature-dependent thermal activity in the relevant pre-runaway window, but an offline DSC experiment cannot uniquely assign the ARC voltage drops to a single reaction.
Mass-loss characterisation

TGA indicates limited mass loss in the pre-failure temperature window

The TGA measurements track temperature and cathode mass versus time and temperature. In the thesis interpretation, the observed mass loss is modest and does not by itself explain Drop 1 as continuous bulk cathode decomposition.

Phase consistency

XRD shows strong pre-failure scan overlap

The quick and long scans overlap closely, with the figure reporting r = 0.9941 and essentially zero angular shift, supporting consistency of the pre-failure cathode phase pattern.

Mechanistic interpretation

What might produce the anomalous voltage response?

Transient electrical connection change

Recovery after the second collapse is difficult to reconcile with a permanently open or permanently hard-shorted state.

Pressure / top-cap protection

The CT-visible CID/pressure-relief architecture provides a physically plausible pathway, but CID motion was not measured directly.

Localized internal short

A localized or evolving short circuit remains a competing explanation and cannot be excluded.

Conclusion

Voltage anomalies may provide an earlier warning channel

The combined evidence shows two discrete pre-runaway voltage anomalies, including a large collapse followed by substantial recovery. The result motivates voltage as a complementary early-warning signal alongside temperature and self-heating rate, while replicated in-situ experiments are still needed to distinguish competing failure mechanisms.
