Anomalous Voltage Responses Preceding Thermal Runaway in Cylindrical Lithium‑Ion Batteries

A study of unusual terminal-voltage drops and recoveries before rapid thermal runaway, and their potential value for earlier battery-failure warning.

Tianli Shi · Department of Chemical Engineering · University College London · Supervisor: Alex Rettie
Explore the findings
Research question

Can voltage reveal failure before temperature does?

The project investigates anomalous voltage responses before thermal runaway in a 100% SOC B18650CX‑20M cylindrical lithium-ion cell.

Key result

The voltage trajectory is non-monotonic

Drop 1 · ≈19.6 h · ≈110 °C

Voltage falls from ≈3.98 V to ≈2.45–2.50 V without a sharp synchronous temperature rise.

Drop 2 · ≈23.6 h · ≈125–130 °C

Voltage falls from ≈2.25 V to ≈0.20 V—about 91% of the pre-event voltage.

Recovery · ≈23.7–26.2 h

Terminal voltage recovers to ≈3.4–3.5 V while self-heating accelerates, inconsistent with a simple permanent hard short.

Interpretation

What might cause the anomalous voltage?

Possible contributors

Side reactions, volatilization, pressure development and transient electrical-contact changes, including possible current-interrupt-device activation.

Unresolved

The measurements cannot uniquely distinguish safety-device activation from a localized or transient internal short circuit.

Safety relevance

Voltage drops and recovery may contribute to early warning when combined with temperature and self-heating rate.
