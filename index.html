Detecting Battery Failure Before Thermal Runaway

An experimental investigation of anomalous terminal-voltage responses in a fully charged cylindrical lithium-ion cell, combining ARC with X-ray CT, DSC, TGA and XRD.

Tianli Shi · Department of Chemical Engineering · University College London · Supervisor: Alex Rettie
Explore the core result ↓View full thesis PDF ↗Download thesis ↓Repository ↗
Research in one sentence: two discrete voltage drops occurred before rapid thermal escalation, and the second collapse was followed by substantial voltage recovery—behaviour that is difficult to explain as a simple permanent hard short and may be useful for earlier battery-failure warning.
Key findings

The electrical signal changed before the failure became thermally obvious.

The central result is a sequence of non-monotonic voltage events that precede rapid thermal runaway.

My contribution

From failure data to a mechanism-focused interpretation.

The project combines experimental testing, signal analysis and materials characterisation to connect terminal-voltage behaviour with plausible internal failure processes.

Experimental failure analysis

Interpreted ARC voltage, surface temperature and self-heating-rate data from slow self-heating through runaway.

Cross-technique characterisation

Used X-ray CT, DSC, TGA and XRD to test structural and thermal explanations.

Mechanism discrimination

Compared transient electrical changes, top-cap protection behaviour and localized internal shorting without treating any unmeasured mechanism as proven.

Core experiment · ARC

Two voltage collapses, then recovery.

The ARC data define the central research problem: both abnormal drops occur before final rapid thermal escalation.

Drop 1 · ≈19.6 h · ≈110 °C

Voltage falls from ≈3.98 V to ≈2.45–2.50 V without a comparably sharp synchronous temperature rise.

Drop 2 · ≈23.6 h · ≈125–130 °C

Voltage falls from ≈2.25 V to ≈0.20 V.

Recovery · ≈23.7–26.2 h

Terminal voltage recovers toward ≈3.4–3.5 V while self-heating accelerates, arguing against a simple permanently hard-shorted state.

Data-quality boundary: post-ADC-overload voltage is treated qualitatively rather than as a reliable quantitative measurement.

Mechanistic interpretation

The evidence narrows the possibilities, but does not force one mechanism.

The exact internal event remains unresolved because the supporting characterisation is not in situ.

Transient electrical connection change

Recovery after the second collapse is difficult to reconcile with a permanently open or permanently hard-shorted state.

Pressure / top-cap protection

The CT-visible CID/pressure-relief architecture offers a physically plausible pathway, although device motion was not measured directly.

Localized internal short

A localized, intermittent or evolving internal short remains a competing explanation and cannot be excluded.

Conclusion

Voltage may provide an additional early-warning channel.

The combined evidence supports voltage as a complementary signal alongside surface temperature and self-heating rate, while replicated in-situ experiments are still required to distinguish competing failure mechanisms.

What is supported

Two discrete pre-runaway voltage anomalies were observed, including a large collapse followed by substantial recovery.

What remains open

The exact internal cause cannot be uniquely assigned from the current offline characterisation set.
